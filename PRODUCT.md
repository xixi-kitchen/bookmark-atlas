# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Bookmark Atlas serves people who rely on browser bookmarks every day, especially users with large or deeply nested bookmark trees. It supports both newcomers who need a clear first-run path and experienced users who want a fast, local-first workspace without losing native browser behavior.

## Product Purpose

Bookmark Atlas turns the browser new tab into a visual workspace that combines native bookmarks, unified search, persistent web embeds, and a full Excalidraw canvas. Success means users can reach a useful bookmark or place real bookmark content on the canvas within a minute, while retaining control over local data, synchronization, and browser-owned bookmarks.

## Brand Personality

Hand-drawn, calm, capable. The interface should feel recognizably Excalidraw-inspired without becoming decorative or playful at the expense of clarity.

## Anti-references

- Forced multi-page tours that block access to the product.
- Generic SaaS dashboards built from nested cards and decorative gradients.
- Persistent toolbars that compete with the canvas or search workflow.
- Hidden destructive behavior, unexpected navigation, or vague synchronization claims.
- Platform-specific wording presented as if it applies to every browser.

## Design Principles

1. Lead users to a real first success instead of teaching every feature.
2. Keep browser-owned bookmarks and Bookmark Atlas canvas state visibly distinct.
3. Prefer progressive, replayable guidance over modal ceremony.
4. Preserve a large, flexible canvas while keeping essential controls compact and stable.
5. Explain privacy, synchronization, and embedding limits at the moment they matter.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

All core flows must remain keyboard accessible, readable in English and Simplified Chinese, and usable with reduced motion enabled. Focus states, semantic labels, contrast, skip controls, and non-color status cues are required. Animation must communicate state and must not be necessary to understand or complete a task.
